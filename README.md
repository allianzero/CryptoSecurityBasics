# CryptoSecurityBasics

Basic crypto security: passwords, seed phrases, phishing, and safe habits. Most crypto losses are not caused by breaking the blockchain — they are caused by tricking the person. This guide teaches you how to be a hard target.

> This guide is educational only, not financial advice. No security practice makes you invincible; these habits sharply reduce your risk.

## 1. The core idea: you are the security system

In traditional banking, the bank can reverse fraud, freeze accounts, and reset passwords. In crypto, transactions are generally irreversible and there is no central help desk that can undo a mistake.

That means your habits are the security system. The good news is that most attacks follow a small set of patterns, and each one has a simple defence.

## 2. Passwords: the basics that actually matter

- **Unique password for every account.** If one site leaks, the others stay safe. Reusing a password across your exchange, email, and wallet defeats every other protection.
- **Long beats clever.** A long passphrase you can remember is better than a short, complicated password you write on a sticky note.
- **Use a password manager.** It generates and stores unique passwords so you only need to remember one strong master password.
- **Protect your email most of all.** Your email can reset almost every other account. Give it the strongest password and the strongest 2FA you have.
- **Never enter a password from a link.** Open the app, or type the address yourself, or use a bookmark you created.

## 3. Two-factor authentication (2FA)

2FA means logging in requires something you know (password) plus something you have (a code or a key).

From strongest to weakest:

1. **Security key (hardware):** a physical device you tap. Hardest to phish.
2. **Authenticator app:** codes generated on your device, such as Google Authenticator or Authy.
3. **SMS code:** better than nothing, but vulnerable to SIM-swap attacks, where an attacker convinces your carrier to move your number to their SIM.

**Do this:** turn on authenticator-app or security-key 2FA on your exchange and email today. Save the 2FA backup codes on paper, in the same safe place as your seed phrase — never in a photo.

## 4. Seed phrases: the one rule

Your seed phrase recreates your entire wallet. Treat it like the master key to a safe.

- Write it on paper, by hand, stored offline.
- Never type it into a website, form, chat, or app that is not the official wallet app restoring a wallet.
- Never photograph it, email it, or store it in a cloud note.
- Never share it with "support", a friend, or anyone offering help. Real support never asks for it.

If anyone asks for your seed phrase, that is the attack. Stop, close the page, and do not continue — no matter how official they look or how urgent they sound.

## 5. Phishing: how fake sites and messages work

Phishing is a fake copy of something real, designed to steal your login, keys, or approval.

Common forms:

- **Fake login page:** identical to the real exchange, reached from a link in an email, post, or DM. You type your password, the attacker captures it.
- **Fake wallet site:** asks you to "connect" or "restore" your wallet, then copies your seed phrase or tricks you into a draining approval.
- **Fake support:** someone DMs you first, offering to fix a problem. Real support never DMs first.
- **Fake app:** a wallet or exchange app in an app store that looks real but steals keys.
- **Fake airdrop or giveaway:** "send 1, get 2 back", or a token that appears in your wallet whose website asks you to connect and claim.

Defences that work:

- Only log in through bookmarks you created or the official app.
- Check the domain character by character. Attackers use lookalike letters and extra words.
- Never log in from a link, even if the sender looks familiar — accounts get hacked and then used to phish their contacts.
- Verify through a second channel. If a "friend" sends a crypto link, message them somewhere else first.

## 6. Approvals: the quiet danger

When you use a decentralised app, it asks your wallet to approve spending a token. That approval can be:

- **Limited:** only a specific amount.
- **Unlimited:** the app can move all of that token, any time, until you revoke it.

Unlimited approvals are convenient and dangerous. A compromised or malicious app with an unlimited approval can drain that token without asking again.

Rules:

- Approve only the amount you need, when the app allows it.
- Read what you are approving. If you do not understand it, do not sign it.
- Regularly review approvals and revoke ones you no longer use.
- Use a separate, low-value wallet for experimenting with new apps. Never connect your main savings wallet to something untested.

## 7. Devices and networks

- **Keep software updated.** Updates patch the vulnerabilities attackers exploit.
- **Lock your devices.** A strong PIN or biometric lock, plus automatic lock after a short time.
- **Be careful on public Wi-Fi.** Avoid logging into exchanges or signing transactions on networks you do not trust. A VPN helps, but it does not make a phishing site safe.
- **Do not install random browser extensions.** Extensions can read pages you visit, including wallet pages. Install only what you need, from verified publishers.
- **Separate devices or profiles when you can.** If you trade actively, consider keeping crypto work separate from casual browsing and downloads.

## 8. The 10 safe habits

1. Unique, long password for every crypto account, stored in a password manager.
2. Authenticator-app or security-key 2FA on exchange and email.
3. Seed phrase on paper only, offline, never photographed or shared.
4. Log in only through bookmarks or the official app, never from a link.
5. Check address, network, and amount before every send.
6. Test with tiny amounts before any new wallet, network, or contract.
7. Read approvals before signing; revoke old and unlimited ones.
8. Keep your recovery email as secure as your exchange account.
9. Verify surprising messages through a second channel before acting.
10. If it promises guaranteed returns or free money for connecting, assume it is a scam until proven otherwise.

## 9. Red flags: stop if you see these

- Urgency plus a link: "act now or your account will be closed".
- A request for your seed phrase, password, or private key.
- A "support agent" who contacts you first.
- Returns that are guaranteed, risk-free, or far above normal.
- A website or app that is almost, but not quite, the real name.
- Pressure to keep the conversation secret, or not to check with anyone else.

Any one of these is enough to pause. Two together mean walk away and verify independently.

## 10. If you think you have been compromised

Act fast, in this order:

1. **Move funds to a fresh wallet** with a brand-new seed phrase, if you believe keys or approvals are exposed. Do this before anything else.
2. **Revoke suspicious approvals** on the compromised wallet.
3. **Change passwords** on your exchange and email, starting with email, and re-check 2FA.
4. **Check your accounts** for transactions, new devices, and changed settings you did not make.
5. **Report it** to the exchange or project if relevant, and warn contacts if your account was used to phish them.
6. **Do not pay a "recovery service".** Anyone charging a fee to recover stolen crypto is almost certainly running a second scam.

## 11. Frequently asked questions

**Is my crypto safe if my password is strong?**
A strong password helps, but it does not protect a leaked seed phrase, a phishing site, or a malicious approval. Security is layers, not one trick.

**Does a VPN make me safe?**
No. A VPN hides your traffic from your network, but it does not stop phishing, malicious approvals, or a compromised device.

**Should I tell anyone my seed phrase "just in case"?**
No. The moment someone else has it, your wallet is no longer only yours. A paper backup in a safe place is the "just in case".

**Are hardware wallets unhackable?**
No device is unhackable, but a hardware wallet removes the most common attack paths by keeping keys offline. Your seed phrase backup remains the weakest link — protect it the same way.

**What is the single most valuable habit?**
Pausing before you sign, send, or share. Almost every scam needs you to act quickly without checking. A 30-second pause defeats most of them.
